PICBENCH.TTP - EWeb's picture timer
===================================

What it does
------------
PICBENCH decodes a JPEG as EWEB.PRG does (libjpeg-turbo) and times it on
your Atari, twice: on the 68030 alone, and with the Falcon's DSP decoding
it while the 68030 hands it the bytes and takes the pixels. So we can see
how much the DSP gives on a real Falcon, against the emulator EWeb was
measured on. It doesn't show the picture.

What it needs
-------------
A Falcon (no FPU needed), with plain TOS, MagiC or MiNT. With TT-RAM (an
accelerator such as the DFB1) it loads and works there. Without a DSP, or
with another program holding it, it times the 68030 only.

How to use it
-------------
1. Copy PICBENCH.TTP and PHOTO.JPG (a made-up landscape, 480 x 320, the
   size of a page's photo) to a folder on your hard disk.
2. Double-click PICBENCH.TTP and press Return in the box that asks for
   arguments: it times PHOTO.JPG at its size, and shown at 192 x 128.
   Or type a JPEG of your own and the size to show it at, such as
     MYPIC.JPG 0 0        at its own size
     MYPIC.JPG 320 200    shown at 320 x 200
3. It writes what it finds on the screen and in PICBENCH.LOG next to it.
   Press a key at the end.

What it prints
--------------
  the DSP: checked
  on the 68030: decode 4635 ms (480x320, JPEG, 8/8), dither 0 ms
  with the DSP: decode 575 ms (480x320, JPEG, 8/8), dither 0 ms

"checked" means the DSP decoded a small known picture as the 68030 does
(EWeb checks it the same way the first time, and leaves a DSP that gets it
wrong alone). "8/8" is the size libjpeg unpacks the picture at before the
picture is made the size it is shown at. The figures above are Hatari's
stock Falcon (16 MHz), which runs about 1.8 times as fast as a real one;
send yours with PICBENCH.LOG.
